PART 2 - the maximum Spearman a PERFECT signal can reach, by share of wrong answers
==============================================================================
Derivation: with a perfectly separating score the ranks are uniform and the label is 1[rank > 1-p];
Cov(rank, label) = p(1-p)/2 and Var(rank) = 1/12, Var(label) = p(1-p), so rho = sqrt(3 p (1-p)).

==============================================================================
PART 4 - how many EXTRA answers from a worse system version are needed to reach the target share
==============================================================================
Real answers: 118. If k of them are wrong and the worse version is wrong a share e of the time,
adding m answers gives (k + e*m) / (118 + m) = 0.3  =>  m = (0.3*118 - k) / (e - 0.3).

real wrong k |   e=0.4: m (total)   e=0.5: m (total)   e=0.7: m (total)
           6 |         294 ( 412)         147 ( 265)          74 ( 192)
          12 |         234 ( 352)         117 ( 235)          58 ( 176)
          24 |         114 ( 232)          57 ( 175)          28 ( 146)

The more the worse version errs (e), the fewer extra answers are needed - and the less it resembles the
real system. 'total' is the number of answers the author labels.
